Stakeholder Management as a Superpower

AJ
Akshar Jothi
Student · Writer · Builder

The PM role is 20% product, 80% people. How to align executives, engineers, and designers without authority.

Product management is ultimately about making decisions with incomplete information. The best PMs aren't the ones with the most data — they're the ones who know what matters and move forward.

The Thinking Framework

Every product decision can be broken down into three questions: Who is this for? What problem does it solve? How will we know if it worked?

Simple questions. Hard answers. That's what makes product management both challenging and deeply rewarding.

1. Problem Space vs. Solution Space

Most teams rush to solutions. They jump from "we need a feature" to "let's build X" without deeply understanding the problem. Great PMs live in the problem space longer. They ask "why" five times. They observe users. They feel the pain themselves.

The solution space is crowded. The problem space is where real innovation lives.

2. The Opportunity Solution Tree

Teresa Torres taught us to think in opportunity solution trees. Start with outcomes, not outputs. Map the opportunities — the unmet needs, the pain points, the desires. Then, and only then, explore solutions.

This prevents the most common product failure: building something nobody wants because you fell in love with your solution before understanding the problem.

3. Decision-Making Under Uncertainty

You'll never have enough data. The market will never give you clear signals. Competitors will surprise you. In this environment, the ability to make good decisions quickly is your superpower.

Use frameworks like RICE (Reach, Impact, Confidence, Effort) for prioritization. Use the Eisenhower Matrix for urgency vs. importance. But ultimately, develop your judgment — that gut feeling that comes from deep user understanding and market awareness.

"The best product managers are comfortable being uncomfortable. They make decisions with 70% of the information and course-correct with the remaining 30%."

Building Conviction

Conviction doesn't come from ego — it comes from evidence. Talk to users. Read support tickets. Watch session recordings. Analyze data. Then, synthesize all of that into a point of view.

Your job isn't to have all the answers. It's to ask the right questions and build enough evidence to make informed bets.

Conclusion

Product thinking is a muscle. It gets stronger with use. Every decision you make, every user you talk to, every experiment you run — it all compounds into better judgment.

Stay curious. Stay humble. Keep building.
