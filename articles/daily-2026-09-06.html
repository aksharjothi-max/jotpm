Stakeholder Management as a Superpower

AJ
Akshar Jothi
Product Manager | Growth Strategy

The PM role is 20% product, 80% people. How to align executives, engineers, and designers without authority.

Strategy without execution is hallucination. The best product teams aren't the ones with the most brilliant strategies — they're the ones that ship consistently and learn from what they release.

The Execution Gap

There's a gap between what teams plan and what they actually deliver. This gap isn't caused by bad engineers or unclear requirements — it's caused by a lack of ruthless prioritization and clear decision-making.

Great execution comes down to three things: clarity, focus, and speed.

1. Clarity of Purpose

Every team member should be able to answer: What are we building? Why are we building it? How will we know if it worked?

If they can't, you have a clarity problem. Clarity comes from great product briefs, clear success metrics, and a shared understanding of the user problem.

2. Focus Through Subtraction

The best teams say no more than they say yes. They know that every feature they don't build is time they can spend on the features that matter.

Use the MoSCoW method: Must have, Should have, Could have, Won't have. Be ruthless about what's "Must." If everything is priority one, nothing is.

3. Speed Through Iteration

Don't wait for perfect. Ship the smallest version that tests your riskiest assumption. Learn from real users. Iterate based on evidence, not opinions.

The best teams ship weekly, sometimes daily. They've built the infrastructure and culture to move fast without breaking things.

"Done is better than perfect. But done with evidence is better than done with hope."

The PM as Conductor

Your job isn't to have the best ideas — it's to create the conditions for the best ideas to emerge and get executed. That means clearing roadblocks, aligning stakeholders, and protecting the team from distractions.

Be the person who makes everyone around you more effective. That's the real PM superpower.

Conclusion

Execution is a team sport. It requires trust, communication, and a shared commitment to outcomes over outputs. Build the right team, give them clarity and focus, and get out of their way.

Ship fast. Learn faster. Repeat.
