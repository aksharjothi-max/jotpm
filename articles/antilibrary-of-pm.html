The Antilibrary of Product Management

AJ
Akshar Jothi
Student · Writer · Builder

Nassim Nicholas Taleb introduced a concept called the "antilibrary" — a collection of books you haven't read. Unlike a regular library (which signals knowledge), an antilibrary signals something more valuable: awareness of how much you don't know.

The smartest people, Taleb argues, have the largest antilibraries. Because every unread book is a reminder that the world is bigger than your current understanding.

This concept applies perfectly to product management. And I think the best PMs should build antilibraries of ideas.

The Paradox of Collected Ideas

As PMs, we collect ideas constantly. User requests. Stakeholder suggestions. Competitor features. Conference talks. Blog posts. App Store reviews. Every day, dozens of potential product ideas compete for our attention.

Most PMs treat their idea backlog as a to-do list. They feel guilty about ideas that linger too long. They pressure themselves to either kill or execute every idea that comes their way.

This is a mistake.

The best idea backlog isn't a list of things to build — it's a library of possibilities. Most of these ideas will never be built. And that's the point.

Why Unbuilt Ideas Create Better Decisions

1. They Expand Your Solution Space

When a user presents a problem, the solution you come up with is limited by the ideas you've been exposed to. A PM with 200 collected ideas will see solutions that a PM with 20 ideas simply cannot imagine.

2. They Teach You What "Good" Looks Like

Reviewing hundreds of ideas trains your intuition. You start to recognize patterns. You develop an instinct for what will work and what won't.

3. They Protect You From Sunk Cost Bias

When you have 200 ideas and pick one, killing that idea doesn't feel like failure — it feels like choosing. But when you have 5 ideas and one fails, the emotional weight is much heavier.

"The goal isn't to build every idea. The goal is to have so many ideas that choosing the right one becomes inevitable."

How to Build Your PM Antilibrary

Step 1: Collect relentlessly. Every idea goes into a single source of truth — a Notion database, a Roam graph, a simple text file. Don't filter. Don't organize yet. Just collect.

Step 2: Review monthly. Once a month, skim through your collected ideas. You'll be surprised how many solve problems you're currently facing.

Step 3: Connect ideas. The magic happens when two unrelated ideas collide. A user request from January + a competitor analysis from June = a breakthrough insight for September.

Step 4: Embrace the unbuilt. Most ideas will never ship. That's not wasted time — it's the cost of developing great judgment.

Conclusion

Stop treating your idea backlog as a to-do list. Start treating it as an antilibrary — a collection of possibilities that makes you smarter through sheer volume, even when most ideas remain unbuilt.

Because in product management, the ideas you don't build are just as valuable as the ones you do.
